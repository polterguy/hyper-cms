Every extraction plan that crosses this desk has three routes out of the
country. This is correct, and we commend it. What is less correct is that two
of the three are roads.

A road can be closed by one bored official and a traffic cone. We have seen it
happen outside Bratislava, and the agent in question spent four hours in a
service station explaining to a waitress that he was a birdwatcher.

## The rule

> Of your three routes, at least one should not touch tarmac.

Water is slow, cold, and almost never watched. An airport has a list. A
harbour has a man called Dmitri who has not looked at a list since 1998 and
would like forty euros.

## What we ask for

When you submit a plan, we now ask for three things per route:

1. Who can close it, and how long that takes them.
2. What it costs in cash, in the local currency, in notes.
3. Whether it works at four in the morning in February.

The third question removes about half of all submitted plans, which tells you
something about when plans are written.

## On the expenses

A boat hired in an emergency is an operational expense. A boat purchased in an
emergency is a capital asset, and you will be asked about it for two years.
Hire the boat.

If the boat is subsequently sunk, that is a separate form, and we are happy to
complete it on your behalf. We have completed eleven.
